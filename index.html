Three tests that decide the tier

1Does the query have demand of its own?If people search the question as a head or mid-tail term (“balloon payment”), it can carry a title tag and a URL. If it's a long-tail variant, it's a passage.
2Does the answer need sections?A comparison table, a worked example, or a “when to / when not to” split means a guide. If the full answer fits in about 80 words, it's a quick answer.
3Is there a shelf to filter?If the answer narrows down which cars to look at, or leads into finance or Sell my Car, the shelf justifies the page. If not, it doesn't.

A question needs to pass all three to become a guide. Two of three means it goes on the cluster hub as a quick answer, linked down to the nearest guide.

What passage ranking does: since 2021 Google can rank a page for a query answered by a single passage, even when the rest of the page covers a broader topic. It is a ranking signal, not a separate index. The cluster hub is what ranks, and a featured snippet can link straight to the passage with a text fragment (#:~:text=).

What it doesn't do: rescue a weak page. A quick answer only surfaces if its cluster hub is strong in its own right, with an intro, its guides, internal links and a tight topic. Treat the hubs as real pages, not bare lists.

A caveat on FAQ markup: since August 2023 Google shows FAQ rich results only for well-known government and health sites, so FAQPage won't earn Cars.co.za an expandable result in Google. It's still worth adding: it's cheap, and it labels each question-and-answer pair clearly for Bing and AI answer engines.

Markup on a cluster hub, quick answers only (FAQPage): 
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "FAQPage",
  "url": "https://www.cars.co.za/answers/selling/",
  "mainEntity": [{
    "@type": "Question",
    "name": "Do I need a roadworthy certificate to sell my car?",
    "acceptedAnswer": {
      "@type": "Answer",
      "text": "Yes. The buyer can't register the car without one..."
    }
  }]
}
